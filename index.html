Understanding the causes and consequences of changing biodiversity across the planet.

The Wright Lab studies how environmental modification by organisms shapes community structure and ecosystem function, and which aspects of biodiversity most regulate that function — working in any system, on any organism, that helps answer the question.

How we work

Any organism, any ecosystem, if the question is right

Full detail on the Research page.

While much of the lab's research has focused on wetland plant communities, we are willing to study any organism and work in any ecosystem to answer questions that interest us — from tropical streams to desert shrublands. Current work spans saltwater intrusion and sea level rise on the North Carolina coastal plain, the ecological consequences of intraspecific trait variability, and what happens when microbial communities collide. Our research program combines observational and experimental approaches with modeling to develop and test hypotheses and build towards synthetic ecological theory.
